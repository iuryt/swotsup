About SWOT's Up

SWOT maps sea surface height in two ~60 km swaths on each side of its track. This is the NASA/CNES Level-2 KaRIn Low Rate SSH, version D: the Expert product on a 2 km grid and, zoomed in, the Unsmoothed one at 250 m: .

Each product is distributed as one file per passage. Here each is a single IceChunk dataset on Arraylake, versioned, cut in chunks of one passage by ~1000 km of track, so a region and dates read only the chunks that cross them, with icechunk and xarray (the Code button gives the lines). Two years of the Gulf Stream (80–50°W, 30–45°N) are 2,031 files (~66 GB) to find and download, or 26 s to read 1 GB of SSHA from the dataset (from a university cluster).

By default the map shows SSHA with the crossover correction, on the DTU25 mean sea surface, minus swot_cloud's cross-track calibration (a bias and a slope per line and side, fitted to KaRIn crossovers and the nadir altimeters after removing the DUACS L4; experimental), without the pixels swot_cloud's editing flag removes (rain cells, sea ice, ships; at 250 m only rain and sea ice for now). Each can be switched off in Display. Zoomed out, the whole globe comes from daily maps of the same product, summed over the chosen days.

Data and credits

SWOT Project, 2023. SWOT Level-2 KaRIn Low Rate SSH Expert (D) [Data set]. CNES. doi:10.24400/527896/A01-2023.015; Unsmoothed (D), doi:10.24400/527896/A01-2023.016. Data from NASA/JPL and CNES, through PO.DAAC.

Changes made here: the files are combined into one dataset, descending passes are turned to run south to north, along/cross-track distances and per-pass geometry are added, and the corrections above are stored as variables (swot_cloud). The map shows averages of the passages on screen pixels; time series are means within a radius. This tool is not endorsed by NASA, JPL or CNES.

The mean sea surface, by default: DTU25MSS Mean Sea Surface, DTU Space (Nilsson, Andersen et al., ESSD 18, 5027, 2026), doi:10.11583/DTU.29412275, licensed under CC BY 4.0; the maps, series and lines add the L2 product's correction from its mean sea surface to it.
